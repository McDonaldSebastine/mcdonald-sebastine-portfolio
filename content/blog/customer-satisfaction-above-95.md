Two hundred-plus customer interactions a week sounds like a volume problem. It's actually a consistency problem. Anyone can give one customer a great experience. Sustaining a 95% satisfaction rating across hundreds of interactions a week requires the good experience to not depend on which day it is, or how tired you are by the fortieth conversation.

## Write the answer once

The first time a question comes up, I don't just answer it — I write the answer down somewhere reusable. Over time this becomes a living playbook, and it means the tenth customer with the same question gets the same quality of answer as the first, without me reconstructing the reasoning from scratch.

## Log everything, immediately

CRM entries that happen "later" don't happen accurately. Logging the interaction while it's still in front of you — not at the end of the day — is the difference between a CRM that reflects reality and one that quietly drifts from it.

## Onboarding sets the ceiling

A customer's satisfaction ceiling is often set in the first interaction, not the tenth. Getting onboarding right — clear next steps, a fast first win, a real human checking back in — does more for long-term satisfaction than any amount of support quality later can make up for.

None of this is complicated. It's just consistently applied, which is the actual hard part.
